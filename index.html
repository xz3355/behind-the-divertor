2 · Systems map

From ore to reactor, in five stages

Colour marks human-rights risk. The orange band is where the public record stops.

China is shifting from mining its own tungsten to importing ore and exporting finished products. China runs the whole chain at home, from mine to APT to powder to finished parts. But its own ore is running down: mining quotas have been cut for three years in a row and ore grades are falling [31]. So it now buys more ore abroad. Concentrate imports rose 75% in the first half of 2025 [32], with Kazakhstan, Myanmar and North Korea the main suppliers; Myanmar alone was about a quarter [33]. At the same time, export controls since February 2025 cut exports of raw and intermediate products such as APT, while China pushes exports of higher-value finished goods [34].

For traceability, this means ore from high-risk places like Myanmar now flows into Chinese refineries and comes out as Chinese-made product, which makes its origin even harder to see.

What this shows. It does not show that tungsten from any one exporter reached a fusion reactor. It shows three things. First, ore from an exporter named in a UN conflict-minerals investigation was still moving through Gulf traders in 2026. Second, Sayari attached no warning to that exporter, so a buyer screening by risk flags alone would have passed it. Third, the route from mine to refiner is visible in trade data, but the route from refiner to fusion component is not visible anywhere.

Halcyon Inc

Refiner · Thai Nguyen, Vietnam

Vonfram Masan (Masan High-Tech Materials)

Runs the APT refinery next to the Nui Phao mine, one of the largest outside China. Vietnam has supplied about a fifth of US tungsten imports [18]. In Sayari it is the single largest buyer of tungsten ore (803 records) and the largest shipper of APT (395 of 723 records).

Sayari: top buyer in HS 2611; top shipper in HS 2841.80

The trail stops

Fusion-grade powder makers

ITER's European tender allowed tungsten monoblocks from only two makers, Japan's A.L.M.T. and China's AT&M, a state-owned firm that has worked on ITER since 2008 [19][20]. Neither publishes where its tungsten comes from. Vietnam ships APT to Japan (169 records), but no record names A.L.M.T. as the buyer.

Other Rwandan flows look different. New Bugarama Mining, the mine Fairphone and Austria's Wolfram Bergbau worked with to source audited, conflict-free tungsten [21], shipped to Hong Kong in July 2026. Banning the whole region would punish mines like this one. Fairphone's own reason for staying was that earlier boycotts left validated mines without buyers and pushed workers into informal mining [21].

5 · Policy brief and tech proposal

A material passport for fusion tungsten

DivertorITER

Each handover signs the record. A missing signature shows exactly where the chain breaks.

1Require audited refiners

Buy only from refiners that pass RMAP and follow OECD due diligence.

2Add origin to every lot

Engineers already track purity by lot [19]. Track the mine too.

3Screen against investigations

Risk flags lag behind: Boss Mining had none. Check UN reports and journalism.

4Stay in hard places

Keep buying from audited Great Lakes mines instead of boycotting the region.

Limit: a passport proves custody, not working conditions, so it works only alongside audits.

What if the rule changes?

New intelligence about a possible regulation arrives before the rule is final. This tool turns that intelligence into a scenario, drops it onto a supply chain, and shows who gets blocked, who is exempt, and how sure we are. Move the assumptions to see how much the answer depends on details that are not decided yet.

What a buyer could do

    The signals behind the transceiver scenario. Some are law, some are only proposed, and some are only reported. The simulator keeps them apart.

    • In force: FCC 26-50 blocks new authorizations for devices containing a logic-bearing component from a Covered List entity, from 13 Oct 2026 [23]. Foreign-made consumer routers (Mar 2026) and power inverters (Jul 2026) need DoW/DHS Conditional Approval for new models [24][25]. InnoLight joined the Pentagon's 1260H list on 8 Jun 2026 [26].
    • Proposed bill: S.5548 would bar InnoLight and Eoptolink transceivers from federal national-security systems after a five-year transition. Introduced 24 Sep 2026, in committee [27].
    • Reported only: an FCC ban on new Chinese transceiver models, possibly starting at 3.2T with a 65% US-BOM exemption. No FCC document yet [28][29]. Chinese makers hold about 60% of datacom transceiver revenue [30].

    What Sayari adds. Sayari flags InnoLight's Hong Kong arm as owned by a 1260H entity, and shows Eoptolink subsidiaries in Thailand, Singapore and the US. That is why the "made in" definition matters. A caution: searching shipments by the name "INNOLIGHT" also returned Foxconn and Amphenol factories in Vietnam, so I used company records, not raw shipment counts.

    Limit: market shares are sourced; US-content shares are illustrative assumptions you can change. The transceiver ban is not law.
